How Does Fox News Frame the WNBA?

Analyzing the right-wing politicization of the Women’s National Basketball Association

I’ve loved basketball and played it (mediocrely) all my life, but I only became a WNBA fan when I moved to Seattle five years ago. (Let’s go, Storm!) Since then, I’ve also become fixated on a question: Why is Fox News obsessed with the WNBA?

The realization dawned on me slowly, especially since I don’t read or watch Fox News myself.

The first clue came at a family gathering while chatting with an older conservative family member. Out of the blue, they started name-dropping WNBA players, praising superstar Caitlin Clark, while making suspect comments about Black players.

This person has never seen a WNBA game in their life. But they do mainline Fox News on cable TV.

The second clue: my web browser. As I started reading more WNBA news, I was recommended more. That made sense. But then I started getting served coverage from, of all places, Fox News.

Headline after headline pitted the league’s most famous straight white star, Clark, against other WNBA players, mainly Black and/or queer women, like Alyssa Thomas, DiJonai Carrington, or Angel Reese.

When it comes to sports, there are, at least seemingly, facts. This team won, and this team lost. This player scored a lot of points. You would expect a lot of similar coverage. But these headlines were clearly different.

So I decided to collect some data. How does Fox News cover the WNBA compared to everybody else?

For years and across topics, Fox News has stoked “hate” and relied on “us vs. them” narratives (e.g., Clark vs. Reese), but that playbook didn’t really extend to the WNBA and its players until more recently.

The Fox News-WNBA Expanded Universe

Seven years ago, Fox News’ WNBA headlines — like many outlets’ — were few and far between. When they did write about the league, it was for run-of-the-mill events: Cathy Engelbert hired as the commissioner, Sue Bird out with an injury, Napheesa Collier claiming Rookie of the Year honors.

Over time and against a backdrop of Black Lives Matter protests, a second Trump presidency, and a historic CBA, Fox News seized on the expanding league as a culture-war target, framing it as both dangerously radical and illegitimate.

Let’s go back in time to see the evolution.

They dramatized conflict between Lebron James and Kelly Loeffler, a former WNBA owner and former senator. In 2021, Loeffler was defeated by Democrat Raphael Warnock, in part thanks to WNBA player advocacy.

Then in 2022, Brittney Griner became the central WNBA focal point, because she was arrested and imprisoned in Russia. The league was suddenly imbricated with broader world politics.

After Griner was freed in a prisoner swap, Fox notably turned to Donald Trump to suggest it was a bad deal.

He plainly devalued her life—and so did Fox by consistently airing this framing.

Clark joined the WNBA in 2024 and fully took the spotlight.

She was often pitted against Reese.

To foster animosity between them, Fox News turned to people like Dave Portnoy (Barstool Sports).

After Sophie Cunningham was traded and joined Clark (and Lexie Hull) on the Fever, forming the “Tres Leches,” she became the second largest connection in 2026.

At the same time, Fox increasingly platformed anti-trans advocates like Enes Kanter Freedom and Riley Gaines.

This brings us to the summer of 2026 when the right-wing politicization of the WNBA burst into the mainstream.

In July, Sophie Cunningham —Clark’s teammate and on-court “bodyguard”—made anti-trans remarks. While she told ESPN that she “got a lot of negative feedback about me hating trans” and clarified that “I never once said that," she also expressed a desire to “protect young girls in a locker room, or young girls in sport who shouldn't have to go against biological men"—using the exact language and hateful agenda items of the anti-trans movement. She later doubled down on those comments.

This inspired right-wing politicians and anti-trans advocates around the world to rally around her, including Vice President J.D. Vance, J.K. Rowling, and Jennifer Sey.

Trans women’s participation in the WNBA has become a non-stop Fox News talking point. On television, they’ve spent more than 13 hours (!) discussing the WNBA this summer alone. It’s worth repeating: no transgender players are currently in the league.

As Fox News pundit Jesse Watters recently said on air: “We have to do this again, really? Someone wore a t-shirt at a WNBA game? This isn’t a news story…. We don’t even cover the NBA. Why are we covering the WNBA?”

When we switch to looking at phrases instead of words, you can see the coverage gap between Fox News and other outlets even more.

Fox consistently uses culture war phrases like ”white privilege” (derogatory) and “biological men” (derogatory, referring to trans women). They talk excessively about players’ comments on social media and hard fouls.

Meanwhile, other outlets talk about actual strategy and game play, like points and rebounds or free agency, or even the WNBA’s historic collective bargaining.

What about language around specific players, like Clark?

This is a juicy question because there is currently a “fight over Caitlin Clark’s soul,” as University of Washington professor Kate Starbird, a leading misinformation researcher and former WNBA player, put it to me. With collaborators, she’s currently studying “outrage bait” around the league on social media.

When Clark joined the league in 2024, the WNBA culture war debates really heated up. Some commentators noted that Clark’s background as a straight, white, Midwestern star may play a role in her broad appeal—a point that conservatives and Fox News pundits have rejected.

But Clark is not a “stable” figure for the conservative or liberal media, Starbird said. And it’s not totally clear what her legacy will be.

One factor in the mix: social media. Starbird points to the growing pressure athletes feel from their large online audiences. “The real risk for internet influencers—a class of celebrities that increasingly includes professional athletes—isn’t ‘falling down rabbit holes,’ but being ‘captured’ and radicalized by their audiences,” Starbird wrote. This is a potential risk for everyone from Clark to Cunningham to Carrington and beyond.

The point is that, at least right now, Clark is often talked about more than she talks. And sometimes the conservative media uses her as a pawn. Let’s take a closer look at the coverage.

“Rejecting” Identity Politics

Fox News vociferously rejects “identity politics” in WNBA coverage. They’ve accused outlets like ESPN, USA Today, and The Athletic of being “racial idolaters” and generally injecting left-wing politics about race, gender, and sexuality into their reporting.

Here’s the irony: Fox News talks more about race, gender, and identity in the WNBA than any other outlet. They’ve mentioned race or racial politics in 16% of their WNBA articles overall, and 23% this year, more than twice as much as The New York Times, USA Today, and ESPN. Meanwhile, Fox mentioned LGBTQ+ topics in 11% of WNBA articles overall and 34% in 2026, far more than any other outlet.

This may be attributed, in part, to the fact that Fox News generally writes more opinion-oriented coverage than the other outlets, but that fact is also telling. Few (if any) outlets write about the WNBA quite so much from a cultural and political perspective.

Across many outlets, discussion of race peaked in 2020, when WNBA players engaged in highly visible protest in support of BLM. They walked off the court during the national anthem and wore BLM and “Say Her Name” shirts on national television.

But unlike other outlets, Fox News continued to make race a focal point after 2020, especially in relation to “race baiting” and “race bullies” around Clark.

If you look at the words most distinctively associated with “race” in Fox’s vocabulary, in fact, it’s race bullies, race idolaters, and race war.

Let’s Not Get Played

This coverage matters. Many Republican politicians, including Trump, regularly consume Fox News and are swayed by it. Fox is also an influential node in a diffuse network of conservative media, including fringe right-wing blogs. According to Open Measures, these blogs published more than 14,000 posts about the WNBA in the last few months, and attracted more than 46,000 comments.

In 2026, Fox discussed the WNBA more than ever, with 27 percent of their online coverage mentioning trans athletes or issues.

As their own pundit Jesse Watters openly said on air, Fox News doesn’t actually care about women’s basketball. Right-wing agitators are using the WNBA to push their own agendas. Fox News is exploiting these phenomenal athletes as lucrative ragebait. And, unfortunately, it seems to be working. In August 2026, OutKick had 13 million visitors, the most in its history, as Fox bragged.

The problem is not that women’s basketball is being made political. Sports are, and have always been, political. (That’s Sports Studies 101.) The problem is that these narratives are so fabricated from the outside and so at odds with the actual politics of most WNBA players. Roxane Gay in fact recently argued that the league should step up and match its players’ historic activism by being a leader in “making space for trans women in women’s sports”.

This would certainly be consistent with the league’s spirit. When I went to my first WNBA game, I was shocked by how inclusive it felt. Black, queer, and POC women were celebrated on and off the court, as well on the jumbotron and in the stands. The dancers were teenaged girls doing sick hip-hop moves in baggy pants. The Storm’s adorably goofy mascot, Doppler, uses they/them pronouns. Fox News-types like to make hell of things like that (and, in fact, they have). But the whole vibe is truly so nice, welcoming, and fun. And the basketball itself is electric.

I don’t know how the WNBA will handle all of this increased attention—from the media, politicians, activists, trolls, new fans, and (unfortunately) my conservative relatives. But I know one thing for sure. The ragebait version of the WNBA is not recognizable to me. From across the political spectrum, we need to see this framing for what it is, and push back against it, lest we all get played.

Data & Methods

Headlines and Articles. I collected any Fox News article (both headline and full text) that mentioned the WNBA or its players from 2019-2026. This corpus includes coverage from Outkick, now owned by Fox and published on their site.

As a comparison, I collected parallel WNBA coverage from ESPN, USA Today, CBS Sports, The Athletic (owned by the New York Times), and Just Women’s Sports.

TV Chyrons. I gathered nine years worth of cable news chyrons from Fox, MSNBC, and CNN, drawing on data from the Internet Archive's Third Eye OCR feed, and inspired by a very cool analysis by Charlie Smart.

Players. In this analysis, I include the 30 WNBA players named most in Fox News articles. For the network, I consider individuals named in 10 or more headlines, and add specific politicized individuals like J.D. Vance, Michelle Obama, and Charlie Kirk.

Fightin Words. The word comparisons use “fightin’ words” aka weighted log-odds with an informative Dirichlet prior (Monroe, Colaresi & Quinn 2008).
