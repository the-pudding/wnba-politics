Analyzing Fox News’ right-wing politicization of the WNBA

By Melanie Walsh, with Jan Diehm

I’ve loved basketball and played it (mediocrely) all my life, but I only became a WNBA fan when I moved to Seattle five years ago. (Let’s go, Storm!) Since then, I’ve become fixated on a question:

Why is Fox News obsessed with the WNBA?

The realization dawned on me slowly, especially since I don’t read or watch Fox News myself.

The first clue came at a family gathering while chatting with an older conservative family member. Out of the blue, they started name-dropping WNBA players, praising superstar Caitlin Clark , while making suspect comments about Black players.

This person has never seen a WNBA game in their life. But they do mainline Fox News on cable TV.

The second clue: my web browser. As I started reading more WNBA news, I was recommended more. That made sense. But then I started getting served coverage from, of all places, Fox News.

Headline after headline pitted the league’s most famous straight white star, Clark , against other WNBA players, mainly Black and/or queer women, like Alyssa Thomas , DiJonai Carrington , or Angel Reese .

When it comes to sports, there are, at least seemingly, facts. This team won, and this team lost. This player scored a lot of points. You would expect a lot of similar coverage. But these headlines were clearly different.

So I decided to collect some data.

I gathered online headlines from Fox News, and six other outlets: the Associated Press, CBS, ESPN, The New York Times, USA Today, and the Washington Post. Then I also looked at television chyrons — those text banners that appear at the bottom of your screen — for Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC. More in the methods.

Meanwhile, Fox leans heavily into culture-war rhetoric and social media outrage, consistently using dog whistle phrases like “white privilege” and “biological men”...

…and tying “Fever star” Clark to their conservative agenda.

But, what about language around specific players?

This is a juicy question because there is currently a “fight over Caitlin Clark’s soul,” as University of Washington professor Kate Starbird, a leading misinformation researcher and former WNBA player, put it to me. With collaborators, she’s currently studying “outrage bait” around the league on social media.

When Clark joined the league in 2024, the WNBA culture war debates really heated up. Some commentators noted that her background as a straight, white, Midwestern star may play a role in her broad appeal—a point that conservatives and Fox News pundits have rejected.

But Clark is not a “stable” figure for the conservative or liberal media, Starbird said. And it’s not totally clear what her legacy will be.

One factor in the mix: social media. Starbird has previously pointed to the growing pressure athletes feel from their large online audiences. “The real risk for internet influencers—a class of celebrities that increasingly includes professional athletes—isn’t ‘falling down rabbit holes,’ but being ‘captured’ and radicalized by their audiences,” Starbird said. Social media dynamics also shape Fox’s coverage. They’re on the hunt for clicks and comments, and they know that subtle hints and coded language can metastasize into bolder claims among regular internet users, provocateurs, and content farms.

The point is that, at least right now, Clark is often talked about more than she talks (at least off the court, and not to the refs). And sometimes the conservative media uses her as a pawn. Let’s take a closer look at the coverage.

You can see the divergent framing even with specific incidents. When The Athletic wrote about a play-off game where Carrington accidentally poked Clark in the eye, for example, they opened by minimizing the incident. They emphasized that Clark herself “downplayed” the poke. It was an accident.

Flip through the coverage to see more diverging moments, including Fox’s glowing portrayal of Cunningham’s fouls next to their racist description of Alyssa Thomas’s “thug-like play.”

By contrast, Fox News opened with a dramatic, descriptive scene of Carrington sticking her “long fingernail” into Clark’s eye, burying Clark’s comments about intentionality paragraphs later.

People get fixated on this kind of story, and they take it into the real world. This has led to scary material harm and racist harassment of mostly Black players. As a white woman, I’ve seen glimpses of this fixation not only from conservative family but from random strangers.

This summer I was waiting at a bus stop, wearing my Storm jersey and headed to watch them play Carrington’s team, the Chicago Sky, when a random man rolled down his truck window and yelled that he hoped there wouldn’t be any dangerous fouls or funny business. (Uh, go away, sir.) It shows how weirdly pervasive this way of seeing the WNBA has become.

“Rejecting” Identity Politics

While Fox News vociferously rejects “identity politics” in WNBA coverage, they’ve accused outlets like ESPN, USA Today, and The Athletic of being “racial idolaters” and generally injecting left-wing politics about race, gender, and sexuality into their reporting.

Here’s the irony: Fox News talks more about race, gender, and identity in the WNBA than any other outlet. They’ve mentioned race or racial politics in 16% of their WNBA articles overall, and 23% this year, more than twice as much as The New York Times, USA Today, and ESPN. Meanwhile, Fox mentioned LGBTQ+ topics in 11% of WNBA articles overall and 34% in 2026, far more than any other outlet.

Does Fox simply write more opinion-oriented coverage, while the other outlets stick to sports? I don’t think so—at least not entirely.

For one thing, this comparison includes any opinion pieces or cultural reporting from The New York Times and the Athletic, ESPN (including from Andscape), the Washington Post, and USA Today. Plus Fox does cover actual game play. But the significant editorialized coverage from Fox is telling in its own right. Few (if any) outlets write about the WNBA quite so much from a cultural and political perspective.

Across many outlets, discussion of race peaked in 2020, when WNBA players engaged in highly visible protest in support of BLM. They walked off the court during the national anthem and wore BLM and “Say Her Name” shirts on national television.

But unlike other outlets, Fox News continued to make race a focal point after 2020, especially in relation to “race baiting” and “race bullies” around Clark .

If you look at the words most distinctively associated with “race” in Fox’s vocabulary, in fact, it’s race bullies, race idolaters, and race war.

Fox News has dramatically accelerated its coverage of LGBTQ+ topics in the last year

on television

Fox’s obsession with race and with the LGBTQ+ community, especially transgender women, are not disconnected. They’re intimately connected, as scholars of Black, feminist, and queer studies have argued for years, especially through concepts like “misogynoir” and “transmisogynoir.”

By dramatizing the danger posed to Clark —and the reverse white racism she supposedly faces—Fox News riles up their base against the broader WNBA, which includes not only many Black women but many gay and queer women, too. According to a recent Atlantic article by Jemele Hill, the league is approximately 70 percent Black, and somewhere between 25 to 35 percent queer.

In a further twist, Fox News often highlights white players who conform to traditional conventions of femininity—like Cunningham Cunningham, Kelsey Plum , or Cameron Brink —and denigrates Black players who do not, advancing conceptions of ideal (straight white femme) womanhood. Drawing these boundaries has only one obvious end: hating women, including trans women.

Of course, Cunningham has also done her part to further this framing with anti-trans remarks. While she told ESPN that she “got a lot of negative feedback about me hating trans” and clarified that “I never once said that," she also expressed a desire to “protect young girls in a locker room, or young girls in sport who shouldn't have to go against biological men"—using the exact language and hateful agenda items of the anti-trans movement. She later doubled down on those comments.

This inspired right-wing politicians and anti-trans advocates around the world to rally around her, including Vice President J.D. Vance, J.K. Rowling, and Jennifer Sey.

Trans women’s participation in the WNBA has become a non-stop Fox News talking point. On television, they’ve spent more than 13 hours discussing the WNBA this summer alone. It’s worth repeating: no transgender players are currently in the league.

In an analysis of chyrons from Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC, Fox was responsible for 96% of the usage of the word “trans” and 90% for “transgender,” totaling over 200 uses.

As Fox News pundit Jesse Watters recently said on air: “We have to do this again, really? Someone wore a t-shirt at a WNBA game? This isn’t a news story…. We don’t even cover the NBA. Why are we covering the WNBA?”

Fox News particularly focuses on fouls and “protecting” Clark

on television

Foul words used more frequently by Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC in television chyrons

One particularly powerful right-wing WNBA narrative—which arguably ties both implicit racism and transphobia together—is about fouls.

From the moment Clark entered the league, conservatives have pushed a story that other players are purposely targeting her on the court, often suggesting because she is white. While Clark has faced real physicality, and there have been demands for improved refereeing across the board, the politicized dimension of this story is pure right-wing spin.

This ramped up to the point that in July 2026 a group of Republican politicians literally sent a letter to the WNBA commissioner, threatening to call for an “investigation by the Department of Justice, the Department of Labor, or the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission."

The Republicans cite, as evidence, the fact that “Clark has been hip-checked, poked in the eye, and struck in the throat during games.” Who emphasized exactly these points for months and months? Fox News. Across more than 200 articles, Fox mentioned these exact words, and fretted about “protecting” Clark from such threats.

Then, in summer 2026, the “protect Caitlin Clark ” conversation gave way to the “protect women and children” from transgender players conversation, a framing that has been weaponized against trans women for a long time.

This coverage matters

Many Republican politicians, including Trump, regularly consume Fox News and are swayed by it. Fox is an influential node in a conservative media machine that feeds right-wing blogs—driving over 14,000 WNBA posts and 46,000 comments in just a few months according to Open Measures.

As their own host Jesse Watters openly said on air, Fox News doesn’t actually care about women’s basketball. They are exploiting world-class athletes as lucrative ragebait — and it works. In August 2026, Fox’s OutKick hit a record 13 million visitors.

The problem is not that women’s basketball is being made political. Sports are, and have always been, political.

The problem is that these fabricated narratives are completely at odds with the players themselves. As writer Roxane Gay recently argued, the league must match its players’ historic activism, making space for trans women while shielding its athletes from “bad-faith attacks, death threats, harassment, and other racial animus.”

Inclusivity is in the league’s DNA. At my first WNBA game, Black, queer, and POC women were celebrated on and off the court. From the dance squad, to the Storm’s non-binary mascot Doppler, the whole vibe is truly so nice and welcoming.

And the basketball is electric.

The ragebait version peddled by conservative media is unrecognizable to anyone actually watching the league. We need to see this framing for what it is and push back, lest we all get played.
