By almost any measure, the WNBA just had its best season ever. On Fox News, they told a very different story.

Why is Fox News obsessed with the WNBA?

By Melanie Walsh, with Jan Diehm

Fox News

Fox New’s fixation on the league dawned on me slowly, especially since I don’t read or watch Fox News myself.

Background graphic shows roughly 40 examples of Fox News’ cable coverage of the WNBA.

The first clue came at a family gathering when an older conservative family member started name-dropping WNBA players, praising superstar Caitlin Clark , while parroting right-wing conspiracy theories about Black players. This person has never seen a WNBA game in their life. But they do mainline Fox News on cable TV.

Background graphic pulls out and plays four videos with chyron headlines: “Why does the left hate Caitlin Clark?”, “Flagrant fouls against Caitlin Clark continue”, “ WNBA suspends Thomas 1 game after punching Clark”, and “Critics say Caitlin Clark is being snubbed by the WNBA.”

The second clue: my web browser. As I started reading more WNBA news, I was recommended more. That made sense. But then stories from, of all places, Fox News started popping up in my recommendations.

Background graphic shows roughly 40 examples of Fox News’ online headlines about Caitlin Clark. Three headlines are pulled out: “Caitlin Clark fans unload on WNBA, Thomas after violent sequence leaves Fever star battered,” “WNBA star who hurt Caitlin Clark’s eye calls for league to take action against Trump administration policies”, and “Dave Portnoy calls Angel Reese a jacka– for jealousy of Caitlin Clark in response to ESPN analyst.”

Headline after headline pitted the league’s most famous straight-coded white star, Clark , against other WNBA players, mainly Black and/or queer women.

Background graphic shows roughly 40 examples of Fox News’ online headlines about Caitlin Clark. Three headlines are pulled out: “Caitlin Clark fans unload on WNBA, Thomas after violent sequence leaves Fever star battered,” “WNBA star who hurt Caitlin Clark’s eye calls for league to take action against Trump administration policies”, and “Dave Portnoy calls Angel Reese a jacka– for jealousy of Caitlin Clark in response to ESPN analyst.”

Fox also picked on players, harping, for example, on how “entitled” and “laughably bad” Angel Reese is, even though Reese was named the 2026 Defensive Player of the Year and reached the Finals in her third season (notably, before Clark).

Background graphic shows roughly 40 examples of Fox News’ online headlines about Angel Reese. Four headlines are pulled out: “Angel Reese embarrasses herself with cringe video, needs to get a grip: WATCH”, “Why does Angel Reese get a pass for acting like an entitled brat over her dumb Caitlin Clark comment?”, “Don’t be fooled, Angel Reese was laughably bad in another blowout loss”, and “Angel Reese dangerously peddles racist conspiracy theory after WNBA coach calls her two words.”

When it comes to sports, there are, at least seemingly, facts. This team won, and this team lost. This player scored a lot of points. You would expect a lot of similar coverage. But this was different. On their website, Fox News even elevated the WNBA right alongside… literal war.

To better understand this level of obsession, I decided to gather some data. I started with articles from Fox News (including OutKick, their dedicated sports arm since 2021) and six other outlets: the Associated Press, CBS (Sports + News), ESPN (+Andscape), The New York Times (+The Athletic), USA Today, and the Washington Post. In total, I collected more than 30,000 articles that referenced the WNBA or prominent players between 2019 and 2026, with some college coverage from before players’ time in the “W” included, too.

I also analyzed thousands of cable TV chyrons — those text banners that appear at the bottom of your screen — from Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC (now MS NOW) since 2017. See more in the methods.

Fightin’ Words

To understand Fox News’ framing, we need to look beyond who they cover to how they cover them.

To analyze this treatment, we’re going to use a method (aptly) called “fightin’ words,” which surfaces the vocabulary most characteristic of each outlet and can handle groups of different sizes. This helps us deemphasize common shared language, like “head coach,” and identify trademark words without picking up rare phrases that have only limited meaning, like “Dan Dakich” (the name of an OutKick writer). It also tells us how confident we should be that usage differences are significant (statistically speaking), using a standardized score called a “z-score.” We’ll refer to it as the “distinctiveness” score.

But, what about language describing specific players? Stars like Clark are often framed very strategically. There is a “fight over Caitlin Clark’s soul,” as professor Kate Starbird, a misinformation researcher and former WNBA player, put it to me. Starbird and collaborators are currently tracking WNBA “outrage bait” on social media, where Clark is often a centerpiece.

When Clark joined the league in 2024, the culture-war media machine really kicked into gear (explore our historical network timeline below). Some commentators suggested that conservative audiences embraced Clark partly because of her background as a straight-presenting, white, Midwestern star — though Starbird notes she isn’t a “stable” figure for the conservative or liberal media. With a few exceptions, Clark is talked about more than she talks — at least off the court, and not to the refs.

To see how that narrative is constructed, we applied the same distinctiveness model to specific grammatical roles: how players are described, how they play, the actions they take, and what is done to them. Sometimes this is Fox News portraying a player directly—“Carrington is a dirty player”—but it can also include other people’s descriptions, or even players discussing themselves—“DiJonai Carrington sent a message to fans who think she plays ‘dirty.’” But remember that Fox likes to perpetuate associations across a wide range of sources and angles, and that they sometimes use particular framings as “ragebait,” i.e., by highlighting Carrington’s claim that she’s not a dirty player, they incite their audience to insist that she is.

Also, because these player-associated terms are less common overall, some of their distinctiveness scores are lower, below true statistical significance (this is denoted with tan bars). We display them because words used even a handful times (or less) can speak volumes. Like, Fox describing Angel Reese’s play as “pathetic” even three times is very telling, regardless of the math—especially when no other outlets use the word to describe a player (outside quoted speech) across tens of thousands of articles.

You can see the divergent framing even with specific incidents. When Carrington accidentally poked Clark in the eye during a playoff game, The New York Times (via The Athletic) began by minimizing the incident, while Fox News opened with a dramatic scene of Carrington sticking her “long fingernail” into Clark’s eye, burying her comments about intentionality paragraphs later.

Flip through the coverage to see more diverging moments, including Fox’s glowing portrayal of Cunningham’s fouls next to their racist description of Alyssa Thomas’s “thug-like play.”

When viewers take that outrage into the real world, the consequences are dangerous. For the league’s mostly Black players, it has triggered intense harassment, hostile confrontations, and even death threats.

As a white woman, I’ve seen glimpses of this fixation not only from conservative family but from random strangers. For instance, a few months ago, I was waiting at a bus stop, wearing my Seattle Storm jersey and heading to watch a game against the Chicago Sky — Carrington’s team — when a man rolled down his truck window and yelled that he hoped there wouldn’t be any dangerous fouls or funny business. (Uh, go away, sir.) It shows how weirdly pervasive this way of seeing the WNBA has become.

“Rejecting” Identity Politics

While Fox News vociferously rejects “identity politics” in WNBA coverage, they’ve accused outlets like ESPN, USA Today, and The Athletic of being “racial idolaters” and generally injecting left-wing politics about race, gender, and sexuality into their reporting.

Here’s the irony: Fox News talks more about race, gender, and identity in the WNBA than any other outlet. They’ve mentioned race or racial politics in 16% of their WNBA articles overall, and 23% this year, more than twice as much as The New York Times, USA Today, and ESPN. Meanwhile, Fox mentioned LGBTQ+ topics in 11% of WNBA articles overall and 34% in 2026, far more than any other outlet.

Does Fox simply write more opinion-oriented coverage, while the other outlets stick to sports? I don’t think so, at least not entirely.

For one thing, this comparison includes any opinion pieces or off-court coverage from other outlets, and Fox does cover actual game play. But the amount of editorializing in Fox’s reporting is telling in its own right. Few, if any, outlets write about the WNBA quite so much through a cultural and political lens.

Let’s explore Fox News’ playbook more.

Race became a Fox News focus when Caitlin Clark joined the league

on television

How Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC split coverage of race in television chyrons

Across many outlets, discussion of race peaked in 2020, when WNBA players engaged in highly visible protest in support of BLM. They walked off the court during the national anthem and wore BLM and “Say Her Name” shirts on national television.

But unlike other outlets, Fox News continued to make race a focal point after 2020, especially in relation to “race baiting” and “race bullies” around Clark .

If you look at the words most distinctively associated with “race” in Fox’s vocabulary, in fact, it’s race bullies, race idolaters, and race war.

Fox News has dramatically accelerated its coverage of LGBTQ+ topics in the last year

on television

How Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC split coverage of LGBTQ+ topics in television chyrons

Fox’s obsession with race and with the LGBTQ+ community, especially transgender women, are not disconnected. They’re intimately connected, as scholars of Black, feminist, and queer studies have argued for years, especially through concepts like “misogynoir” and “transmisogynoir.”

By dramatizing the danger posed to Clark — and the reverse white racism she supposedly faces — Fox News riles up their base against the broader WNBA, which includes not only many Black women but many gay and queer women, too. According to a recent Atlantic article by Jemele Hill, the league is approximately 70 percent Black, and somewhere between 25 to 35 percent queer.

In a further twist, Fox News often highlights white players who conform to traditional conventions of femininity — like Cunningham , Kelsey Plum , or Cameron Brink — and denigrates Black players who do not, advancing conceptions of ideal (straight white femme) womanhood. Drawing these boundaries has only one obvious end: hating women, including trans women.

Of course, Cunningham has also done her part to further this framing with anti-trans remarks. While she told ESPN that she “got a lot of negative feedback about me hating trans” and clarified that “I never once said that," she also expressed a desire to “protect young girls in a locker room, or young girls in sport who shouldn't have to go against biological men" — using the exact language and hateful agenda items of the anti-trans movement. She later doubled down on those comments.

This inspired right-wing politicians and anti-trans advocates around the world to rally around her, including Vice President J.D. Vance, J.K. Rowling, and Jennifer Sey.

Trans women’s participation in the WNBA has become a non-stop Fox News talking point. On television, they’ve spent more than 13 hours discussing the WNBA this summer alone. It’s worth repeating: no transgender players are currently in the league.

In our analysis of chyrons from Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC, Fox was responsible for 96% of the usage of the word “trans” and 90% for “transgender,” totaling over 200 uses.

As Fox News pundit Jesse Watters recently said on air regarding Lynx coach Cheryl Reeve wearing a “Trans Kids Belong” shirt: “We have to do this again, really? Someone wore a t-shirt at a WNBA game? This isn’t a news story…. We don’t even cover the NBA. Why are we covering the WNBA?”

Fox News particularly focuses on fouls and “protecting” Clark

on television

How Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC split coverage of fouls in television chyrons

One particularly powerful right-wing WNBA narrative — which arguably ties both implicit racism and transphobia together — is about fouls.

From the moment Clark entered the league, conservatives have pushed a story that other players are purposely targeting her on the court, often suggesting it’s because she is white. While Clark has faced real physicality, and there have been demands for improved refereeing across the board, the politicized dimension of this story is pure right-wing spin.

This ramped up to the point that in July 2026 a group of Republican politicians literally sent a letter to the WNBA commissioner, threatening to call for an “investigation by the Department of Justice, the Department of Labor, or the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission."

The Republicans cite, as evidence, the fact that “Clark has been hip-checked, poked in the eye, and struck in the throat during games.” Who emphasized exactly these points for months and months? Fox News. Across more than 200 articles, Fox mentioned these exact words, and fretted about “protecting” Clark from such threats.

The conservative fixation on “protecting” Clark easily merged with another familiar right-wing narrative: “protecting women and girls” from transgender athletes. Both relied on the same rhetorical device, casting women's sports as a hostile environment in need of cultural rescue.

Fox News’ framing increasingly relies on “Us vs. Them”

Fox News’ playbook revolves around stoking hate and creating “Us vs. Them” conflict. But, their WNBA coverage wasn’t always this way.

In 2019, the network covered the WNBA like any other outlet: injury reports, trade rumors, and little else. While the players had always been outspoken, the 2020 Covid bubble season transformed their activism into organized political muscle that conservative media couldn’t ignore.

After players dedicated the season to Breonna Taylor with "Say Her Name" shirts, Atlanta Dream co-owner and GOP Senator Kelly Loeffler publicly objected. The league responded by mobilizing to help unseat her — and Fox News found a prime target. That hostility hardened in 2022 over the Griner Brittany Griner prisoner swap, which commentators attacked as a weak concession for an unpatriotic athlete.

By the time Clark arrived, the playbook was fully written: weaponize her stardom into culture-war rage bait, framing her as an innocent target under siege by a resentful, "woke" establishment.

This coverage matters

It’s tempting to dismiss this coverage as drivel. But unfortunately, it matters.

Fox News feeds an influential media machine that also includes prolific right-wing blogs and fringe online content farms. Even more concerning, many Republican politicians, including President Trump, regularly consume Fox News and are swayed by it.

Fox News

In fact, Trump mentioned the WNBA several times at rallies in battleground states in the lead-up to the 2026 midterm elections. He fawned over Cunningham’s looks and also castigated the league to loud boos.

Background graphic shows roughly 40 examples of Fox News’ cable coverage of the WNBA. Two videos of Trump talking about the WNBA are pulled out.

As their own host Jesse Watters openly said on air, Fox News doesn’t actually care about women’s basketball. They are exploiting world-class athletes as lucrative ragebait. And it’s working, at least as a business strategy: in August 2026, Fox’s OutKick hit a record 13 million visitors.

The problem is not that women’s basketball is being made political. Sports are, and have always been, political. The problem is that these fabricated narratives are completely at odds with the players and with the WNBA’s historic activism.

Background graphic shows roughly 40 examples of Fox News’ cable coverage of the WNBA. Two videos of Fox News talking about covering the WNBA are pulled out.

The Democrats’ commanding victory in the 2026 U.S. midterm elections is a hopeful sign that Americans are rejecting some prominent conservative narratives, including anti-trans rhetoric.

But there’s still more work to do. Washington (my home state) passed an initiative that bans students assigned male at birth from competing in girls’ sports and requires all athletes to verify their sex via a health care provider. Similar measures passed in Colorado, Nebraska, and Arizona.

We can’t let the WNBA get used as a Trojan horse for anti-trans legislation, or for any kind of bigotry.

So let’s change the channel. As writer Roxane Gay recently argued, the league should match its players’ progressive advocacy and make space for trans women while also shielding its athletes from “bad-faith attacks, death threats, harassment, and other racial animus.”

Inclusivity is in the league’s DNA. At my first Seattle Storm game, I remember how different the WNBA felt from other pro sports. Women — many of them Black, POC, and queer — are celebrated on and off the court. And all kinds of people are uplifted and embraced. From the hip-hop youth dance squad to the Storm’s adorable non-binary mascot, the whole vibe is truly so nice and welcoming. And the basketball is dynamite.

The version of the WNBA peddled by the conservative media — the ragebait W — is unrecognizable to anyone actually watching the league. In this pivotal moment of the league’s growth, we need to see this framing for what it is and push back, lest we all get played.
