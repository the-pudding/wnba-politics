Why is Fox News obsessed with the WNBA?

“From the logo!”

By Melanie Walsh, with Jan Diehm Turn on sound

Fox News

I’ve loved basketball and played it (mediocrely) all my life, but I only became a WNBA fan when I moved to Seattle five years ago. (Let’s go, Storm!) Since then, I’ve become fixated on this question. The realization dawned on me slowly, especially since I don’t read or watch Fox News myself.

The first clue came at a family gathering when an older conservative family member started name-dropping WNBA players, praising superstar Caitlin Clark , while making suspect comments about Black players. This person has never seen a WNBA game in their life. But they do mainline Fox News on cable TV. Turn on sound

The second clue: my web browser. As I started reading more WNBA news, I was recommended more. That made sense. But then I started getting served coverage from, of all places, Fox News.

Headline after headline pitted the league’s most famous straight white star, Clark , against other WNBA players, mainly Black and/or queer women.

Fox also picked on players, harping, for example, on how “entitled” and “laughably bad” Angel Reese is, even though Reese was named the 2026 Defensive Player of the Year and reached the Finals in her third season (notably, before Clark).

blank

When it comes to sports, there are, at least seemingly, facts. This team won, and this team lost. This player scored a lot of points. You would expect a lot of similar coverage. But these headlines were clearly different.

On their website home page, Fox even elevated the WNBA to be an issue on par with… literal war.

To better understand this level of obsession, I decided to gather some data. I started with articles from Fox News (including OutKick, their dedicated sports arm) and six other outlets: the Associated Press, CBS (Sports + News), ESPN (+Andscape), The New York Times (+The Athletic), USA Today, and the Washington Post. I grabbed articles that referenced the WNBA or prominent players, so some college coverage is included, too. I also looked at cable TV chyrons — those text banners that appear at the bottom of your screen — from Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC. (See methods for more.)

Fightin’ Words

To fully grasp Fox News’ framing, we need to go a step further: looking not just at who they cover, but how. To quantify that difference, we’re going to use a method (aptly) called “fightin’ words,” which surfaces the vocabulary most unique to each outlet.

Other outlets focus on actual basketball, emphasizing game strategy with terms like points, rebounds, steals, blocks, and free agency .

Meanwhile, Fox leans heavily into culture-war rhetoric and social media outrage, consistently using dog whistle phrases like “white privilege” and “biological men”...

…and consistently tying “Fever star” Clark to their conservative agenda.

But, what about language describing specific players? Stars like Clark are often framed very strategically. There is a “fight over Caitlin Clark’s soul,” as professor Kate Starbird, a misinformation researcher and former WNBA player, put it to me. Starbird and collaborators are currently tracking WNBA “outrage bait” on social media, where Clark is often a centerpiece.

When Clark joined the league in 2024, the culture-war media machine really kicked into gear (see our historical timeline below). Some commentators suggested that conservative audiences embraced Clark partly because of her background as a straight, white, Midwestern star—though Starbird notes she isn’t a “stable” figure for the conservative or liberal media. With a few exceptions, Clark is talked about more than she talks—at least off the court, and not to the refs.

You can see the divergent framing even with specific incidents. When Carrington accidentally poked Clark in the eye during a playoff game, The Athletic began by minimizing the incident, while Fox News opened with a dramatic scene of Carrington sticking her “long fingernail” into Clark’s eye, burying her comments about intentionality paragraphs later.

Flip through the coverage to see more diverging moments, including Fox’s glowing portrayal of Cunningham’s fouls next to their racist description of Alyssa Thomas’s “thug-like play.”

People get fixated on this kind of story, and they take it into the real world. This has led to scary altercations, abuse, and harassment of mostly Black players.

As a white woman, I’ve seen glimpses of this fixation not only from conservative family but from random strangers. For instance, a few months ago, I was waiting at a bus stop, wearing my Seattle Storm jersey and heading to watch a game against the Chicago Sky—Carrington’s team—when a man rolled down his truck window and yelled that he hoped there wouldn’t be any dangerous fouls or funny business. (Uh, go away, sir.) It shows how weirdly pervasive this way of seeing the WNBA has become.

“Rejecting” Identity Politics

While Fox News vociferously rejects “identity politics” in WNBA coverage, they’ve accused outlets like ESPN, USA Today, and The Athletic of being “racial idolaters” and generally injecting left-wing politics about race, gender, and sexuality into their reporting.

Here’s the irony: Fox News talks more about race, gender, and identity in the WNBA than any other outlet. They’ve mentioned race or racial politics in 16% of their WNBA articles overall, and 23% this year, more than twice as much as The New York Times, USA Today, and ESPN. Meanwhile, Fox mentioned LGBTQ+ topics in 11% of WNBA articles overall and 34% in 2026, far more than any other outlet.

Does Fox simply write more opinion-oriented coverage, while the other outlets stick to sports? I don’t think so, at least not entirely.

For one thing, this comparison includes any opinion pieces or off-court coverage from other outlets, and Fox does cover actual game play. But the amount of editorializing in Fox’s reporting is telling in its own right. Few, if any, outlets write about the WNBA quite so much through a cultural and political lens.

Let’s explore these topics more.

Across many outlets, discussion of race peaked in 2020, when WNBA players engaged in highly visible protest in support of BLM. They walked off the court during the national anthem and wore BLM and “Say Her Name” shirts on national television.

But unlike other outlets, Fox News continued to make race a focal point after 2020, especially in relation to “race baiting” and “race bullies” around Clark .

If you look at the words most distinctively associated with “race” in Fox’s vocabulary, in fact, it’s race bullies, race idolaters, and race war.

Fox News has dramatically accelerated its coverage of LGBTQ+ topics in the last year

on television

Fox’s obsession with race and with the LGBTQ+ community, especially transgender women, are not disconnected. They’re intimately connected, as scholars of Black, feminist, and queer studies have argued for years, especially through concepts like “misogynoir” and “transmisogynoir.”

By dramatizing the danger posed to Clark —and the reverse white racism she supposedly faces—Fox News riles up their base against the broader WNBA, which includes not only many Black women but many gay and queer women, too. According to a recent Atlantic article by Jemele Hill, the league is approximately 70 percent Black, and somewhere between 25 to 35 percent queer.

In a further twist, Fox News often highlights white players who conform to traditional conventions of femininity—like Cunningham Cunningham, Kelsey Plum , or Cameron Brink —and denigrates Black players who do not, advancing conceptions of ideal (straight white femme) womanhood. Drawing these boundaries has only one obvious end: hating women, including trans women.

Of course, Cunningham has also done her part to further this framing with anti-trans remarks. While she told ESPN that she “got a lot of negative feedback about me hating trans” and clarified that “I never once said that," she also expressed a desire to “protect young girls in a locker room, or young girls in sport who shouldn't have to go against biological men"—using the exact language and hateful agenda items of the anti-trans movement. She later doubled down on those comments.

This inspired right-wing politicians and anti-trans advocates around the world to rally around her, including Vice President J.D. Vance, J.K. Rowling, and Jennifer Sey.

Trans women’s participation in the WNBA has become a non-stop Fox News talking point. On television, they’ve spent more than 13 hours discussing the WNBA this summer alone. It’s worth repeating: no transgender players are currently in the league.

In our analysis of chyrons from Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC, Fox was responsible for 96% of the usage of the word “trans” and 90% for “transgender,” totaling over 200 uses.

As Fox News pundit Jesse Watters recently said on air regarding Lynx coach Cheryl Reeve wearing a “Trans Kids Belong” shirt: “We have to do this again, really? Someone wore a t-shirt at a WNBA game? This isn’t a news story…. We don’t even cover the NBA. Why are we covering the WNBA?”

Fox News particularly focuses on fouls and “protecting” Clark

on television

Foul words used more frequently by Fox News, CNN, and MSNBC in television chyrons

One particularly powerful right-wing WNBA narrative—which arguably ties both implicit racism and transphobia together—is about fouls.

From the moment Clark entered the league, conservatives have pushed a story that other players are purposely targeting her on the court, often suggesting because she is white. While Clark has faced real physicality, and there have been demands for improved refereeing across the board, the politicized dimension of this story is pure right-wing spin.

This ramped up to the point that in July 2026 a group of Republican politicians literally sent a letter to the WNBA commissioner, threatening to call for an “investigation by the Department of Justice, the Department of Labor, or the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission."

The Republicans cite, as evidence, the fact that “Clark has been hip-checked, poked in the eye, and struck in the throat during games.” Who emphasized exactly these points for months and months? Fox News. Across more than 200 articles, Fox mentioned these exact words, and fretted about “protecting” Clark from such threats.

Then, in summer 2026, the “protect Caitlin Clark ” conversation gave way to the “protect women and children” from transgender players conversation, a framing that has been weaponized against trans women for a long time.

This coverage matters

It’s tempting to dismiss this coverage as drivel. But unfortunately, it matters.

Fox News feeds an influential media machine that also includes prolific right-wing blogs and fringe online content farms. Even more concerning, many Republican politicians, including President Trump, regularly consume Fox News and are swayed by it.

Fox News

In fact, Trump mentioned the WNBA several times at rallies in battleground states in the lead-up to the 2026 midterm elections. He fawned over Sophie Cunningham’s looks and also castigated the league to loud boos. Turn on sound

As their own host Jesse Watters openly said on air, Fox News doesn’t actually care about women’s basketball. They are exploiting world-class athletes as lucrative ragebait, and it’s working: in August 2026, Fox’s OutKick hit a record 13 million visitors.

The problem is not that women’s basketball is being made political. Sports are, and have always been, political. The problem is that these fabricated narratives are completely at odds with the players and with the WNBA’s historic activism. Turn on sound

So let’s change the channel. As writer Roxane Gay recently argued, the league should match its players’ progressive advocacy and make space for trans women while also shielding its athletes from “bad-faith attacks, death threats, harassment, and other racial animus.” Turn on sound

Inclusivity is in the league’s DNA. At my first WNBA game, I remember how different it felt from other pro (mostly men’s) sports. Women—many of them Black, POC, and queer—are celebrated on and off the court. And all kinds of people are uplifted and embraced. From the hip-hop dance squad to the Storm’s adorable non-binary mascot, the whole vibe is truly so nice and welcoming. And the basketball is electric. Turn on sound

blank

The version of the WNBA peddled by the conservative media—the ragebait W—is unrecognizable to anyone actually watching the league. We need to see this framing for what it is and push back, lest we all get played.
